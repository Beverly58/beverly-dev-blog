## Reflecting on the System Beyond Features (LO3)

As the Fandom Passport project became more technically structured through DDDs and relational database planning, this week focused on evaluating the broader responsibilities of the system beyond visual interaction and reward mechanics.

While the project is designed as a playful and immersive fan experience, I began considering the ethical and usability implications of building a system that tracks participation, ranks users, and visualises fan identity through collectible stamps and VIP levels.

One important concern is the potential pressure created by visible progression systems. In many online fan communities, ranking systems can unintentionally encourage unhealthy competition or obsessive participation. Because of this, I decided that the passport system should avoid displaying public numerical rankings between users. Instead, the design focuses on personal collection progress and self-expression rather than direct competition.

## Accessibility and Inclusive Interaction

Another area I reflected on was accessibility. The skeuomorphic passport interface relies heavily on visual metaphors such as stamps, embossed layouts, and barcode elements. While these visuals strengthen the identity of the project, they may create usability issues for some users.

To improve accessibility, I plan to:
- maintain high colour contrast between interface elements
- provide text labels alongside decorative stamp icons
- ensure keyboard navigation compatibility
- avoid interactions that rely only on hover states
- maintain responsive layouts across desktop and mobile devices

These considerations align with the AA accessibility standards outlined in the assignment brief.

## Technical Performance and Scalability

From a technical perspective, I also considered how the database structure may scale over time. Since fandom events and achievements constantly change, the relational structure designed in previous weeks allows new stamps and tasks to be added without restructuring the entire system.

Using HTMX partial rendering also helps reduce unnecessary page reloads, which may improve perceived responsiveness and support the assignment requirement regarding loading performance.

## Evaluation Planning

Although the prototype is still under development, I identified several methods that could be used to evaluate the final application:

- usability testing with fans familiar with K-pop communities
- observation of task completion flows
- accessibility testing for keyboard and screen-reader compatibility
- evaluating whether users understand the stamp system intuitively
- collecting feedback about emotional engagement and collectability

This week shifted my focus from “what the system does” to “what responsibilities come with designing the system.” I believe this perspective is important when creating community-based platforms that influence participation, identity, and digital behaviour.
